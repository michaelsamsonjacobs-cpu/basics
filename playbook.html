BASICS Ready-Gate Engineering & Diligence Standard

4 Hard Lessons From Shipping Production AI

Most student teams lose investor meetings or enterprise pilots over avoidable technical and narrative mistakes. Before BASICS opens the Ready Gate to Berkeley's investor network — or sends you on to Y Combinator, SkyDeck, or Techstars — we hold every team to four battle-tested rules.

Rule 01 · Technical Honesty

Nothing Exists Until Independently Benchmarked Against a Simple Baseline

A training loss curve going down or a cherry-picked Loom demo is not a product. Technical partners at top seed funds and enterprise buyers now run 5-minute sanity checks during diligence.

  • Hold out unseen scenarios: Keep at least 20% of real customer workflows or physical environments completely untouched during development.
  • Beat the naive baseline first: Compare your model against the simplest deterministic rule, SQL lookup, or physics equation. If a 20-line heuristic ties your neural net, fix the data before pitching accuracy numbers.
  • Separate eval from training: Never share preprocessing code between training and evaluation — shared bugs manufacture fake 99% metrics.
Rule 02 · Mission-Critical Architecture

Anchor AI Inside Domain Guardrails & Deterministic Safety Interlocks

If you sell into regulated or physical industries — electric utilities (like BASICS/YC alum Evardi), healthcare, robotics, defense, or financial infrastructure — buyers will never trust an unbounded black-box prompt.

  • Domain Prior + Bounded Residual: Structure critical predictions as output = domain_baseline + gate * learned_residual so the system degrades gracefully to known physics or business rules under distribution shift.
  • Deterministic Hard Interlocks: Enforce safety invariants, compliance limits, and authorization gates in deterministic, unit-tested code outside the model.
  • Version-Lock What Works: Once a model or workflow passes live customer field tests, hash-lock it and run new candidates in shadow mode before promoting.
Rule 03 · Cleanroom IP Hygiene

Zero Copyleft (AGPL-3.0) & Zero Non-Commercial Dataset Poison Pills

One careless pip install in Week 2 can stall a Series A or kill an enterprise procurement six months later.

  • Audit dependencies on Day 1: Avoid AGPL-3.0 libraries (such as copyleft vision wrappers or viral server frameworks) in your core commercial runtime. Stick to permissive Apache-2.0, MIT, and BSD stacks.
  • Track data provenance: Never train commercial weights on academic CC-BY-NC ("Non-Commercial") datasets or unconsented customer data. Log exact dataset lineage alongside every saved checkpoint.
Rule 04 · Ready-Gate Narrative & Capital Discipline

Evidence Over Slide Theater — Respect the Investor Graph

Berkeley's alumni investor graph is a scarce community trust asset. BASICS never blasts cold lists; we send curated, double-opt-in introductions one at a time when your evidence earns it.

  • Customer discovery before code bloat: Bring real operator notes, killed hypotheses (Week 3 Productive Failure), and live usage or LOI evidence.
  • Clarity wins rooms: Keep slides visual and concise — real product screenshots, quantitative baseline comparisons, honest bottom-up wedge sizing, and zero buzzword walls.
  • Combine dilutive & non-dilutive leverage: Pair smart seed capital with university grants, NSF I-Corps, SBIR/STTR non-dilutive R&D, and paid enterprise pilots to protect founder ownership.
